Intel Arc B580 firmware debugging — part 3 (CH341A physical flash)

Continuing from the opromdata & VBT dissection — physical SPI dump, binary comparison, and the working firmware write.

CH341A preparation — killing the 5V line

Stock CH341A programmers are not safe to use on modern SPI flash chips without modification. Or so they say, Im doing it anyways. 3.3v feels safer than 5v when its the flashers says 3.3v operation. The board supplies 5V on the data lines by default(MOSI and MISO I believe), the SPI chip on the b580 runs at 1.8v, so after modification, you will need another 1.8v adapter to go on top

The fix was to destroy the 5V trace feeding the ZIF socket with a blade, then run a jumper wire from the 3.3V rail to the point where the 5V line used to feed. After the mod, MOSI and MISO were confirmed clean at 3.3V before the SOIC8 clip touched anything on the card. I obviously didnt have a jumper, so I just wrapped the 3.3v and 5v header with some cable organizer wires.

Dumping the SPI flash

The SPI flash on the ASRock B580 Challenger was accessed via SOIC8 clip with the card fully powered off and disconnected from the system. The dump was read three times. All three reads diffed as identical, confirming a clean capture. The Asrock variant of the b580 uses a GigaDevice 8MB 25LB64ESIG

A second physical ASRock B580 Challenger was sourced and the same procedure was repeated under identical conditions to produce another bios dump as a known-good reference.

Binary comparison: b580true1.bin vs b580working.bin

Both images share the same overall SPI layout. The following regions are byte-for-byte identical. Im still on the edge of whether to share it with the firmware matrix yet, but likely claude probably already has it lol between the two dumps:

  • FPT partition structure
  • fwcode version — 21.1098 on both
  • FITB partition — all 0xFF on both, meaning neither card has a populated backup firmware slot
  • Primary VBT block — 7,287 bytes, identical in both images

The divergence is entirely in the opromdata region. In b580working.bin, the OROM manifest entry (a-OROM.man) sits at the expected offset within the $CPD partition, correctly aligned. In b580true1.bin, that same manifest entry is shifted 16 bytes forward from where it should be.

The OROM code build also differs between the two: build 1051 in the working image versus build 1065 in the corrupted one. This suggests the card had received a partial or interrupted opromdata update at some point that shifted the manifest without completing cleanly — consistent with the card's history of occasional PCODE failures before things became permanent.

Critically, both opromdata slots on the corrupted card (A and B) carried the same 16-byte shift. The two slots were identical to each other. There was no clean copy anywhere on chip to recover from.

Writing the working firmware

The working firmware from b580working.bin was written to the SPI chip via CH341A and verified with a full readback diff. The write confirmed byte-for-byte identical to the source image.

Post-flash state

The card stabilised enough to reach desktop and run light workloads at 720p low settings. Artifacting and crashes appeared under heavier load at 1080p medium settings. FurMark produced a DMA violation BSOD on Windows with ReBAR enabled.

At this point the opromdata mismatch hypothesis was substantially weakened. The card now held clean firmware from a confirmed working donor, yet instability under load persisted. Diagnosis shifted toward a hardware failure explanation, with VRAM as the primary suspect based on the idle framebuffer corruption pattern and the nature of the DMA violation.
